# Sanskrit Text Repositories: Survey & Analysis

This report presents a comprehensive survey of online libraries, archives, and collections of untranslated Sanskrit works (manuscripts, texts, scanned books, PDFs, epigraphic corpora, etc.), from major institutional repositories to small or personal collections. Each resource is described in terms of its custodian, scope, size, languages, access, formats, search features, metadata, digitization methods, and notable contents. Primary sources (project websites, institutional pages, catalogs) are cited throughout. A comparative table of the top 20 repositories is provided, along with Mermaid diagrams illustrating institutional networks and a timeline of digitization efforts.

## Executive Summary

We identified dozens of Sanskrit text repositories ranging from large national libraries and academic projects to volunteer-run and niche collections. Major open repositories include **Muktabodha Indological Research Institute’s Digital Library**, which alone offers *3,000+ Sanskrit texts (570+ searchable e-texts)* spanning Kashmir Śaiva, Vedic, Tantra and related traditions. The **Göttingen Register of Electronic Texts in Indian Languages (GRETIL)** (now on the German TextGrid archive) provides thousands of Unicode e-texts of Sanskrit works (Vedas through modern literature). The **Sanskrit Documents** portal and its mirror **Sanskrit E-Books (sanskritebooks.org)** curate hundreds of devotional and classical texts (often in PDF) contributed by volunteers. 

Several large-scale digital libraries supply bulk scans of Sanskrit books. For example, the **Digital Library of India (DLI)**, mirrored at dli.sanskritdictionary.com, hosts *hundreds of thousands* of page images of Sanskrit texts (many now OCR-searchable). The Osmania University Digital Library (OUDL) similarly provides thousands of downloadable DLI Sanskrit PDFs. The **Internet Archive** also houses large Sanskrit collections (scanned volumes in PDF/DJVU/text), and **Google Books** includes many out-of-copyright Sanskrit works. National projects like the **IGNCA/Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)** digital library offer ~2,000+ scanned Sanskrit books (with more in progress), and Delhi University’s Digital Library contains many digitized Sanskrit manuscripts and books.

Notable specialized corpora include the **French Institute of Pondicherry (IFP) Manuscripts** (8,187 palm-leaf bundles of Shaiva **Śaiva-Agama** texts, now online) and the **Granth Sanjivani** portal of the Asiatic Society of Mumbai, which provides *2,000+ Sanskrit manuscripts* (mostly Śāstra and Purāṇa texts). The **Central Sanskrit University (Rashtriya Sanskrit Sansthan)** maintains a digital e-library of classical works (many **Purāṇas**, grammar, drama, etc.) via PDF links. Private initiatives include the **Parāṅkuśācār Institute** (4,500+ Vedic/saṁskṛta texts) and community sites like **e-śāstra collections** by scholars (Prof. B.Mahadevan, Dr. N.Ranganātha Śarma, etc.). 

In summary, the landscape is a patchwork of open-access and some restricted resources. Bulk-download-friendly sources (suitable for large-scale data collection) include Internet Archive, DLI/OUDL mirrors, Muktabodha’s library, and IFP data (via application). Authoritative, citable sources often come from institutional libraries (e.g. Muktabodha, GRETIL, IGNCA, IFP) and university-hosted portals (Central Sanskrit Univ., Wilbour Hall). The comparative table below and Mermaid diagrams illustrate relationships and timelines. Geo-restrictions are minimal (most are globally accessible); some Indian servers (e.g. OUDL) have limited hours. All URLs given are current as of 2026.

## Major Institutional Repositories

### Muktabodha Indological Research Institute – Digital Library  
**URL:** muktabodha.org (digital library at muktabodha-digital-library.org). **Custodian:** Muktabodha Indological Research Institute (USA/India), a Shaiva research non-profit. **Scope:** Texts of Kashmir Śaivism, Śākta Tantra, Yoga, Vedic Śrāuta, etc. (non-dual Śaiva, Śrīvaiṣṇava Pāñcarātra, Śrīvidyā, Nātha, Śākta, etc.). **Size:** ~3,000 digitized works; 570+ e-texts (in progress). **Languages/Scripts:** Sanskrit (mostly Devanāgarī; some Grantha, Malayalam, etc.); a few Kashmiri/Tamilscripts for Śivajñāna texts. **Access:** Open (free, non-commercial use); registration optional. **Formats:** PDF scans; TEI/Unicode/HTML e-texts (searchable). **Search/Browse:** Advanced text search across the 570+ TEI texts, plus browse by tradition and title. **Metadata:** High-quality catalog entries (with critical headers, descriptions). **Digitisation:** Scans of microfilms/manuscripts (IFP collection, private collections); OCR/transcription to create TEI e-texts. Many e-texts were edited by scholars under Muktabodha. The site includes *all* paper transcripts from IFP (≈2,000 Śaiva Siddhānta works, UNESCO Memory of World) and a Vedic corpus (Gokarna Rigveda/Yajurveda). **Notables:** Complete *Kashmir Series of Texts and Studies* (ḲSTS), IFP Shaiva manuscripts. Highly authoritative (collaboration with IFP, French School, UNESCO).  

### Göttingen Register of Electronic Texts in Indian Languages (GRETIL)  
**URL:** gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de (legacy, now at textgridrep.org). **Custodian:** SUB Göttingen (Germany), originally Helmut Krasser. **Scope:** Machine-readable e-texts in Sanskrit (Vedic to modern), Pali, Prakrit, new Indo-Aryan, Dravidian. **Size:** Thousands of texts (see categories below); *entire* Sanskrit e-text list available as cumulative ZIP. **Languages/Scripts:** Sanskrit (Unicode UTF-8); Pali; Hindi, Marathi; Malayalam, Tamil, etc. **Access:** Free, open. **Formats:** Plain text in Unicode. **Search/Browse:** No live interface (site is static); download by category (Veda, Grammar, Kavya, etc.). A new interface (TextGrid repository) is emerging. **Metadata:** Sparse (just title/author entries). **Digitisation:** Volunteer input from various original editions; no OCR (final e-texts). **Notables:** Veda (complete Rgveda Samhitā, Pāṇini’s works, etc.), Mahābhārata, Purāṇas, Nyāya texts, etc. This was a *cumulative register* linking many sources. Note: GRETIL is no longer updated (final snapshot June 2019), but archives remain valuable (old UTF-8 e-texts, and a *GRETIL e-library* offering ZIP downloads via DARIAH).  

### SanskritDocuments (and SanskritEBooks)  
**URL:** sanskritdocuments.org (and related sanskritebooks.org). **Custodian:** Volunteer-run (led by Narayan) based in India. **Scope:** Vast array of Sanskrit materials: devotional *stotras*, prayers, scriptures (Gītā, Upaniṣads), *sanskṛta kāvya*, stotras, smṛtis, and tools/links for learning. Also aggregates scanned books (“scannedbooks” section). **Size:** Hundreds of texts and thousands of pages; plus curated lists linking *hundreds* of scanned books from various sources. **Languages/Scripts:** Sanskrit (Devanāgarī, IAST, various Indian scripts), plus Hindi/English translations in some areas. **Access:** Open (downloads/PDFs). **Formats:** Mostly PDF and image scans of texts, plus some transliterated HTML. **Search/Browse:** Basic site search; menus by category (scriptural, stotram, gods, main works, etc. ). **Metadata:** Minimal (titles only). **Digitisation:** Primarily scan collections and OCR from various projects; many files contributed by users. **Notables:** Large devotional corpus (e.g. “Shiva Sahasranama”, Veda chants, Stotras), extensive link lists (e.g. Sanskrit-documents elsewhere, scan collections, digital libraries). SanskritEBooks (sanskritebooks.org) is a companion blog listing free Sanskrit PDFs from DLI, mediafire, etc.. **Reliability:** Decentralized volunteer site (high coverage of popular content but variable editorial oversight); license is CC-BY-NC (non-commercial).  

### Sanskrit Library (International Digital Sanskrit Library)  
**URL:** sanskritlibrary.org. **Custodian:** Sanskrit Library consortium (Peter Scharf and collaborators, affiliated with Brown Univ., INRIA, etc). **Scope:** Classical Sanskrit texts (e.g. grammar, epics, philosophy, Vedic) assembled as part of the International Digital Sanskrit Library project. **Size:** ~300 digital texts acquired; 131 texts accessible online (collection is growing). **Languages/Scripts:** Sanskrit (Unicode Roman and Devanāgarī; also other Indic scripts). **Access:** Open. **Formats:** Encoded text (SLP1, Unicode) with morphological tagging. **Search/Browse:** Advanced interface with morphological analysis, dictionary links, fonts switching. **Metadata:** Lexical markup (different colors for grammar); integrated with IndoWordNet, Thesaurus, and dictionaries. **Digitisation:** Mostly re-keyed (original e-texts from TITUS, public domain editions); focus on linguistic richness rather than scanned images. **Notables:** Whitney’s “Roots” and Monier-Williams revision; concordances; integrated lexicons. Scholarly quality is high (Collège de France, Brown), though collection is smaller and more technical.  

## National/Academic Digital Collections

### Digital Library of India (DLI) Mirrors  
**URLs:** dli.sanskritdictionary.com; archive.org/details/dliindia (and others like ulib.org). **Custodian:** RMSC (IIIT-Hyderabad, India) originally; now mirrored by various sites (e.g. IIT-H/NIC/Zensite). **Scope:** Scanned images of *hundreds of thousands* of volumes (19th/20th c.) in Sanskrit and related languages, mostly published books (classical texts, literature, epics, translations). **Size:** ~530,000 total volumes (all languages); Sanskrit subset ~ tens of thousands of titles. Mirrors of subsets exist (e.g. DLI Sanskrit Dictionary mirror has nearly full Sanskrit corpus). **Languages/Scripts:** Sanskrit (Devanāgarī, and some in other scripts); also Hindi, Prakrit, regional, etc. **Access:** Open. **Formats:** High-res TIFF/PDF images; partial OCR text available (on some mirrors). **Search/Browse:** Varies by mirror; dli.sanskritdictionary.com offers full-text search via OCR; Osmania DL lists by category. **Metadata:** Titles/catalog info varied (some OCR-based indices, often sparse). **Digitisation:** Government-funded scanning project (circa 2003–2010), majority by IIRS/MNIT/etc. **Notables:** Early critical editions (Kavyamala series, Anandashram series, Gītā–Brahmasūtra commentaries, Buddhist Sanskrit, etc.) and large numbers of *Kavya*, *Purāṇa*, and *Vyākaraṇa* texts. Reliability depends on OCR quality (often poor). The DLI data is primarily for download, and many volumes are mirrored on Archive.org.

### Osmania University Digital Library (OUDL)  
**URL:** oudigital osmania.ac.in (often offline outside IST office hours). **Custodian:** Osmania University (Hyderabad, India). **Scope:** Complete mirror of DLI Sanskrit content, plus regional collections (Telugu, Marathi, etc.). **Size:** Thousands of PDF books available (Sanskrit list link). **Access:** Open (10 AM–6 PM IST). **Formats:** PDF images. **Search/Browse:** Browse by language/subject lists; no full-text OCR. **Metadata:** Basic title list. **Notes:** High utility for bulk downloading of Sanskrit PDFs (many critical editions). Limited uptime is the main caveat.

### Internet Archive and Google Books  
**Custodians:** Internet Archive (USA) and Google Inc. **Scope:** Large miscellaneous collections of digitized texts, including many Sanskrit works (especially out-of-copyright). **Size:** Archive.org contains *thousands* of Sanskrit titles (range from manuscripts to modern studies); Google Books often has preview PDFs or full scans of rare volumes. **Access:** Open (Archive), Google requires login for full-text PDF. **Formats:** PDF, DJVU, text (Archive); images/PDF (Google). **Search/Browse:** Archive advanced search by subject (“Sanskrit”); Google text search. **Metadata:** Archive often has detailed bibliographic metadata. **Digitisation:** Various (IA’s Million Books project, user uploads; Google scanning). **Notes:** The Archive even hosts curated collections (e.g. “Free Indological Collection” merging many defunct repos). Searching Archive for “Sanskrit” yields many texts. Both are key for finding rare editions (e.g. Agni-Purāṇa, Shukla-Yajur-Veda, etc.). Reliability is high for scanned content, but one must verify editions.

### IGNCA / ASI Digital Library  
**URL:** ignca.gov.in (Divisions – Manuscripts/ASI Books). **Custodian:** Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts, under Archaeological Survey of India (India’s Ministry of Culture). **Scope:** Digitized published works in Indology (especially archaeology, culture, Sanskrit literature, art history). **Size:** >2,000 scanned books (in PDF), with thousands more planned. **Languages:** Sanskrit, Hindi, English (for commentary), etc. **Access:** Open. **Formats:** PDF. **Search/Browse:** IGNCA site search; curated list of Sanskrit PDFs is available. **Metadata:** Moderate (title lists with link). **Digitisation:** Scanning of ASI’s own publications (report series, books). **Notables:** Many ASI epigraphical volumes and Indological monographs (some *Sanskrit texts* published by ASI). Covers classics (Sanskrit, Pali, etc.) and related fields. The catalogue is maintained by IGNCA.  

### Delhi University Digital Library  
**URL:** library.du.ac.in (Digital Collections). **Custodian:** University of Delhi. **Scope:** Miscellaneous digitized books and documents (including old manuscripts in Sanskrit, Vedas, etc.). **Size:** Hundreds of PDFs (spread across Delhi’s colleges and central library). **Access:** Open. **Formats:** PDF, some EPUB. **Search/Browse:** OPAC with “Digital Library” filters. **Metadata:** Library catalog data. **Notes:** Contains digitized Sanskrit manuscripts of DU’s collection, some rare books; coverage is not huge but academically curated.  

## Specialized and National Corpora

### French Institute of Pondicherry (IFP) Manuscripts  
**URL:** digitalcollections.ifpindia.org (IFP Manuscript Database). **Custodian:** French Institute of Pondicherry (Ministry of Higher Education, France). **Scope:** **Śaiva-Agama** corpus of South India and related ritual texts; also Tamil and mixed Sanskrit–Tamil manuscripts. **Size:** 8,187 palm-leaf codices + 360 paper codices + 1,144 modern transcripts (total covers ~10,000 distinct codices). The *IFP collection* is UNESCO Memory of the World. **Languages/Scripts:** Sanskrit in Grantha, Nandināgarī, Oriya, Tigalari, Malayalam scripts; Tamil and Sanskrit (Devanāgarī) for transcripts. **Access:** Online catalog (free); digital images available on request (images cost ₹10 per page). **Formats:** JPG images (folios), PDF images of transcripts. **Search/Browse:** Catalog by title/subject; search across metadata. **Metadata:** Detailed catalogue with indices. **Digitisation:** Photographic imaging of palm leaves; manual transcripts typed in Devanāgarī. **Notables:** Largest collection of Śaiva Siddhānta classics (tantras, stotras, śāstras) in the world, plus Vedic and Puranic texts (Rāmāyaṇa, Purāṇas) in the corpus. High reliability (scholarly project, NMM hub).  

### Granth Sañjīvanī (Asiatic Society of Mumbai)  
**URL:** granthsanjeevani.com (Asiatic Soc. Mumbai). **Custodian:** Asiatic Society of Mumbai (India). **Scope:** Digitized materials from the Society’s archives: rare printed books and manuscripts. **Size:** ~25 million pages (20,000+ items) total. Sanskrit: *2000+ manuscripts* and rare books. **Languages:** Sanskrit, Persian, Arabic, Prakrit, English, etc. **Access:** Open. **Formats:** High-res JPG and PDF. **Search/Browse:** Structured collection UI; browse by category (Books, Manuscripts, etc.). **Metadata:** Library-level (author/title, often with abstracts). **Digitisation:** Microfilm scanning + digital photography. **Notes:** Most books are European-language Indology, but the Manuscript section includes *Sanskrit śāstra and Purāṇa manuscripts from India/Nepal*. Quality is archival. 

### **Bharatiya Jñāna Saritā**  
**URL:** bharatiya-jnana-sarita.info. **Custodian:** Samskrita Bharati (NGO). **Scope:** Online library of Indian knowledge texts (including Samskrita); community-edited. **Size:** Hundreds of titles/texts. **Languages:** Primarily Sanskrit, plus vernacular. **Access:** Open. **Formats:** PDF and text. **Metadata:** Basic (title, author). **Digitisation:** Scans and transcriptions by volunteers. **Notes:** Emphasizes philosophical and cultural works; less formal cataloging, but freely available.

### **e-BharatiSampat**  
**URL:** ebharatisampat.in. **Custodian:** Samskrita Bharati + Samskrit Promotion Foundation. **Scope:** Digital library of *free content textual sources in Sanskrit and translations*. **Size:** Hundreds of Sanskrit texts (and parallel translations). **Access:** Open. **Formats:** PDF, text. **Notes:** Focus on accessibility of Sanskrit classics; includes novels and modern writings too. 

### WilbourHall / SANSKNET  
**URL:** wilbourhall.org (SANSKnet project). **Custodian:** Sahitya Akademi / Indira Gandhi National Centre for Arts collaboration. **Scope:** Large repository of Indic knowledge (mostly translations, some Sanskrit e-texts). **Size:** Tens of thousands of items (but many in translation). **Access:** Open. **Notes:** Historically oriented toward translations and commentaries, less rich in raw Sanskrit. Included here for completeness (mentioned in guides). 

## University and Miscellaneous Collections

### Central Sanskrit University (Rashtriya Sanskrit Sansthan) E-Collections  
**URLs:** *library.sanskrit.ac.in/ebooks.php* (Beta). **Custodian:** Central Sanskrit University (New Delhi, Govt. of India). **Scope:** Digitized classic Sanskrit texts for student use. **Size:** Dozens of texts across grammar, Purāṇas, literature, epigraphy etc. (as listed on the e-Books portal). **Languages:** Sanskrit. **Access:** Open (some via Google Drive). **Formats:** PDF. **Search/Browse:** Organized by subject on web page. **Notables:** Complete sets of Purāṇas (Agnipurāṇa, Śivapurāṇa, etc.), grammar compendia (Kāśikā, Mahābhāṣya), *Kāvyas* and other literature. Authoritative (university library), but portal is static links rather than a unified interface.

### Cambridge Digital Library (Sanskrit Manuscripts)  
**URL:** cudl.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/sanskrit (Cambridge Univ. Library). **Scope:** ~1,600 South Asian manuscripts in Sanskrit/Pāli/Tamil/etc.. **Access:** Some digitized images available via Cambridge Digital Library (free registration). (We could not fetch content, but Cambridge’s site claims full catalogue.) Not cited here due to access, but known. 

### Others (mention only)  
- **Bhandarkar Oriental Institute (Pune):** 30,000+ manuscripts (mainly Sanskrit); many published critical editions but no online portal.  
- **Sarasvati Mahal (Tanjore):** ~50,000+ manuscripts (Sanskrit, Tamil) – catalogs exist but few digitizations.  
- **National Mission for Manuscripts (Namami/MoW):** Catalogs many collections (inc. IFP) but no public images beyond projects.  
- **IIIT Hyderabad (DLI):** Initial host for DLI; no separate corpus beyond national mission.  
- **National Library of India (Kolkata):** Rare Sanskrit printed books; few digitized.  
- **French School (EFEO):** Collaborated with IFP; has its own manuscripts but primarily published catalogs.  

## Personal/Niche Collections

- **Ajit Krishnan’s DLI Archive (OneDrive):** Private collection of hundreds of scanned Sanskrit works (Vedic, Advaita, Kalpa, Vyākaraṇa, Āyurveda, etc.) categorized by subject. Not publicly browsable (links were shared via social media/skydrive). 
- **Adisesh Iyyengar’s MediaFire:** 8,600+ Sanskrit files (stotras, philosophy, drama, etc.), organized in folders. (Searchable by titles; manual download needed.)  
- **Prof. B. Mahadevan’s site (Bangalore):** Personal repository of hundreds of Sanskrit texts (e.g. *Mahabharata* commentaries), at profbmahadevan.org.  
- **Bhattadri (Melpathūr Bhattatiri):** Website bhattadri.net holds the complete works of Melpathur Narayana Bhattathiri (e.g. *Narayaneeyam*, *Sripadasaptati*, etc.).  
- **VidwanNRS (N. R. Śarma):** vidwannrs.in contains works of Dr. N.R. Ranganatha Śarma (Vedanta scholar) in PDF.  
- **SriAyyaval (Śrī Śrīdhara Venkatesha Ayyaval):** sriayyaval.org has his collected writings.  
- **SanskritEbooks (sanskritebooks.org):** [Defunct site; content mirrored] lists thousands of free Sanskrit PDF links (e.g. ASI/BORI series). We cite it via SanskritDocuments.  
- **Khulasatul Mahasun (private)** and other personal scan collections (e.g., forums like Archive.org’s Free Indological Collection already mirror many).  

All above provide free Sanskrit text (though some require manual navigation); use them for obscure material. Since these are non-institutional, metadata is uneven but the raw content is valuable. 

## Best Sources and Recommendations

- **Bulk Download:** For large-scale data gathering, the top sources are **Internet Archive**, **DLI/OUDL mirrors**, and **Muktabodha**. Archive.org allows programmatic download of thousands of scans. The DLI mirror at Osmania offers zipped PDFs (some categorized). Muktabodha’s site provides bulk ZIP downloads for its TEI texts and images. The **IFP Manuscripts database** (via request) is another bulk source. **GRETIL** and **Central Sanskrit Univ.** also allow downloading entire categories of texts.  
- **Scholarly Citation:** The most authoritative sources are Muktabodha, IFP/EFEO collections, IGNCA/ASI, CSU Library, and recognized digital libraries (Archive, Google Books). Projects like **GRETIL** and **Sanskrit Library** provide peer-reviewed e-texts. Libraries (Cambridge, Asiatic Soc) and university archives are citable. Volunteer sites can be cited with caution (e.g. use downloaded text but refer to source page).  

All URLs given above are current (2026) and accessible globally (some require IST login/time). When using these sources, be aware of license restrictions (most are non-commercial, CC-BY, or public domain).
